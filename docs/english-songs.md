# English songs (experimental)


The app also sings English songs **without changing anything in Spanish**: the render of "Los pollitos dicen" and of the consonant catalog comes out identical, sample by sample, to the previous version. It stays marked as experimental until it is tested with native speakers. It turns on automatically when the UltraStar file says `#LANGUAGE:English`, or by hand in "Sound → Song language". "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star" (public domain) is included as a demo.

- **Pronunciation.** English is not written the way it is pronounced, so the **CMU Pronouncing Dictionary** is used (about 134,000 words in ARPAbet with stress; npm package `cmu-pronouncing-dictionary`, ISC license). It is downloaded from jsDelivr the first time an English song is chosen, like the MediaPipe model. The demo song's words are included so it works offline. A word that is not in the dictionary is read with the Spanish rules and counted in the song details ("N words not in the dictionary"; the list is printed in the browser console).
- **Syllables and notes** (`js/logic/english.js`). Each word is split into syllables (maximal onset principle with the possible English clusters) and the syllables are distributed among the notes that make up the word in the file, according to where each vowel falls in the letters. If a note has more than one syllable ("fire", "of the") the stressed one stays; if there are notes left over, they keep the previous vowel. In melismas (`~`) the diphthong glides only on the last note.
- **Vowels.** 12 vowels and 5 diphthongs of American English with the averages from **Hillenbrand et al. (1995)** (45 men and 48 women; the original data is at github.com/santiagobarreda/hillenbrand_et_al_1995). With "Vowels: Cantor Digitalis" the men's values are used; with "Soprano", the women's. Unstressed vowels are reduced to [ə]. The diphthongs (eɪ aɪ aʊ oʊ ɔɪ) hold the first vowel and glide to the second in the last part of the note. Each vowel has a calibrated gain to sound at the level of its Spanish counterpart.
- **The face.** The camera does not see the tongue, so it cannot tell 12 vowels apart (/i/ from /ɪ/, /ɑ/ from /ʌ/). The mouth picks the **family** (the same five positions that are calibrated: a e i o u) and the **song's lyrics decide the exact vowel**: if the family matches, the English vowel sounds with its diphthong. The lane and the area under the mouth show the vowel in IPA (ɪ, ʌ, aɪ…). Calibration and MediaPipe detection do not change.
- **Calibration for English speakers.** Calibration stores mouth shapes, not sounds, so the mechanism is the same (rest and five positions) and detection does not change. What changes, with the interface in English, is how each shape is requested: with a word whose vowel is the most representative and can be held without gliding: *ah as in father*, *eh as in bed* (not *day*, which is a diphthong and closes), *ee as in see*, *oh as in go* (held without closing towards *oo*: English has no pure /o/ and *saw* does not work because in many American accents it sounds the same as *father*) and *oo as in food*. The vowel bars are labeled with the same words (ah eh ee oh oo). With the interface in Spanish, calibration works as always, because the shapes are the same.
- **To do: extended calibration.** Each family groups "secondary" vowels with intermediate mouths: /ɪ/ (*sit*) with *see*, /æ/ (*cat*) with *bed*, /ʌ/ (*cup*) with *father*, /ɝ/ (*her*) with *go*. It needs to be measured with native speakers whether the camera confuses them with another family. If it does, the proposed solution is an optional extended calibration with those four extra mouths (ten steps in total), stored as a second shape of their family, so the family is recognized by the closest shape. Diphthongs are not calibrated: you sing with the starting mouth and the engine does the glide.
- **New consonants** (in `js/logic/articulation.js`, next to the Spanish ones): **aspirated** p t k at the start of a word or stressed syllable (VOT of 55–70 ms; unaspirated after s), [θ] (*think*), [ð] (*the*, using the approximant that already existed), v, z, [ʒ] (*measure*) and [dʒ] (*judge*) with voicing under the noise, [h], [ŋ] (*sing*), the **English r [ɹ]** (approximant with a low F3, no tongue tap), the final **dark l** and the **American flap t** [ɾ] (*water*, *little*), which is the Spanish single r.
- **Browser-free render:** `node tools/render.mjs song <file.txt> <output.wav> --dictionary <index.js of the cmu-pronouncing-dictionary package>` and `node tools/render.mjs catalog-en <output.wav>` (new consonants, vowels and diphthongs, with a timing .txt).

**Limitations.** The coda sounds with a single consonant (the most audible one: in *hands* the z sounds), so long final clusters are simplified. The recorded fragments of p t k and of the fricatives come from the Rioplatense dataset: English aspiration is synthetic. [ɹ] and [θ] are synthetic and should be checked with English speakers. Offline, only the demo's words are known. Hillenbrand's formants are from speech, not singing (unlike the soprano table).
